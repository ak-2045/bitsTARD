The Dual Degree Route

A 5-year M.Sc. + B.E. program that many students choose to enter BITS at a comparatively lower cutoff — with a chance to get branches like CS or MnC later based on first-year CGPA.

Is taking a Dual Degree actually worth it?

It’s not just “one extra year”. There’s CG pressure, branch competition, internships, flexibility, and a very different experience depending on what someone wants from college. FAQ

Answers sourced from the BITS community — no sugarcoating.

What's the difference between ECE / EEE / ENI? +
Honestly, nothing significant. All the Phoenix students will have the entire 2nd year in common, as well as a few 3rd year courses. All the branches differ by at most 3–4 courses, which you can also take as electives. Moreover, their average packages differ by about 2 lakhs at max, further strengthening my point. I'd recommend treating all of them as equivalent and prioritising them on the basis of campus.
Is taking a Dual worth it? +
Depends on person to person. If you have enough capital and are a sincere student who doesn't think of studying as a burden, it should be worth it, as you'll get branches like CS/MnC at a much lower entrance exam score. All this provided you get close to 8 CGPA in the first year, which isn't very hard, but that doesn't mean you'll slack off. Assuming that you did slack off, the cutoff for ENI should be around 6.8 for Goa, so you should at least end up with an electronics branch, if not anything better.
How many dualites get CS? +
Well, Goa has close to 340 dualites as of now, out of which around 90–95 will get CS. Around 270–290 (or even more) dualites will end up with circuital branches, which is a great percentage of people.
How easy is it to score 8+ in the first year? +
Fairly easy; you need a B grade (usually awarded at average +10–15) in all your subjects to be an 8-pointer. Some courses, such as GenChem (which is just JEE Chemistry), are fairly easy to score an A in, whereas labs are mostly an easy A- if not A, so it shouldn't be very challenging to score a CG good enough for CS in the first year (again, provided you're a sincere student, which you should be able to gauge by yourselves now that you've been through the JEE rat race).
Is Electronics difficult? +
Yes, compared to other branches on campus, the workload is higher. The grading is also a little unfair and more on the worse side (but it might change for Goa since the HoD was recently changed). But maintaining a good CG (8+ if not 8.5+) is, again, not a mammoth task. I know a few seniors in Goa phoenix who have good enough CGPA in spite of being in a Phoenix branch. It all depends on how serious you are and your interest towards academics.
So, an MSc or Electronics? +
Well, if you think you'd like another chance at getting a branch like CS, have enough capital to afford the 5 year fees comfortably, and are willing to work for it in the first year, then MSc is a great option. Worst case you'll end up with an electronics branch, which is still great RoI. If you prefer enjoying the first year of your college life and being stress-free or aren't willing to pay that much money, Electronics is the way to go.

Mech is not “lite”

Mechanical sounds chill until you realize half your week disappears into labs, workshops, submissions, and surprise quizzes. Trying to manage coding prep alongside that is basically a test of character.

MBA route is underrated

A good MBA after 1–2 years of work experience can genuinely change your career trajectory. But yes, if your dual upgrades into CS/MnC/ECE, suddenly everyone starts saying “bro you’ve already won in life”.

4-yr B.Tech 15–20 LPA job → 1–2 years work-ex → Strong MBA profile becomes much easier → Start post-MBA around 25–30 LPA
5-yr B.Tech + MSc 20+ LPA placements → 1–2 years work-ex → MBA becomes optional instead of necessary → Career can easily be amplified to 30+ LPA post MBA
Dual Degree If your dual slides into CS / MnC / ECE, it can completely change your career trajectory. At that point, many people skip the MBA route entirely because the opportunities are already strong enough.
Hard work matters. Luck matters too. Sometimes the market is great. Sometimes companies disappear overnight. Sometimes your dream company hires 2 people and both have been doing competitive coding since class 8. Plan properly, but always keep backup options.
